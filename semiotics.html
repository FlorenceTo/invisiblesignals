Multispecies Semiotics of Synthetic Sensing 001

Florence To  ·  Duration: 1:02 min

In the Wadi Auja area, Jericho, large clusters of black kites, white storks, and other birds swarmed above an undesignated wasteland. Discarded matter had been pushed into the ground, forming a damp, decomposing substrate of bacteria, insects, and moisture. Cracks in the softened earth released swarms of flies, while nearby electrical infrastructure introduced another layer of activity into the landscape.

The recordings approach this site as a multispecies sensing environment in which birds, insects, microbial processes, waste, soil, and electromagnetic infrastructures participate in the production of signals. Electromagnetic frequencies were recorded from fissures in the ground while simultaneously documenting the movement of bird flocks overhead. For several hours, the birds repeatedly circled and descended, tracing patterns of attraction linked to food availability and ecological conditions. The visual models generated from these recordings provide another mode of observation. They document atmospheric conditions from the same day while making visible how sound propagates through air, tracing vocal exchanges between birds and the presence of electromagnetic emissions in the surrounding environment.

Florence To is an artist and researcher of Hong Kong diasporic heritage based in London. Their transdisciplinary practice engages sound, installation, sculpture, and field research to investigate the relationships between frequency, infrastructure, and memory. Examining how technological systems mediate and reconfigure relations within more-than-human environments, they engage with visual and acoustic ecology and phenomenology, positioning listening as a methodological framework through which technological systems, environments, and multispecies life can be understood.
